## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can own the full lifecycle of bias testing — from metric selection through governance to production monitoring — not just run a one-off check. At senior level, a bias testing programme must be systematic, repeatable, and embedded in the engineering workflow.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My organisation used ML models in three consequential contexts: credit risk scoring, insurance premium calculation, and recruitment shortlisting. Each had been developed by separate teams with different approaches to fairness — one team had done no fairness testing, one had tested only for gender parity, and one had tested gender and age but used accuracy as the fairness metric rather than equalised odds. I was asked to design a unified bias testing programme that applied consistently across all three.

**Task:** I needed to define a programme that was technically rigorous, legally appropriate across all three use cases, and operationally sustainable.

**Action:** I structured the programme across five components. Component 1 — use case risk classification: I worked with legal and compliance to classify each model by its regulatory exposure. Credit scoring and insurance were subject to the Equality Act 2010 and FCA guidance; recruitment was subject to employment equality law. This determined the protected characteristics we were required to test and the legal standard (equalised odds for credit and insurance, demographic parity as a floor for recruitment). Component 2 — metric selection framework: I documented the appropriate fairness metric for each model type, with legal rationale. For credit and insurance: equalised odds (equal true positive and false positive rates across groups) — because the cost of a false negative (denying credit to a qualified applicant from a protected group) was a legal and ethical risk. For recruitment: demographic parity as a minimum floor, equalised odds as the aspiration. I also required that accuracy be reported separately per demographic group — not just overall — because a model with 90% overall accuracy can have 70% accuracy for a minority group. Component 3 — proxy variable audit: For every model, I required a feature importance analysis identifying any feature with a correlation above 0.3 with a protected characteristic. Each identified proxy was assessed: was its use justified by predictive value that could not be achieved without it? Could it be replaced by a less correlated feature? This was signed off by a legal reviewer, not just the data science team. Component 4 — release gate: Every model release required a fairness evaluation report showing all protected group metrics, proxy variable findings, and a comparison against the previous version. Any metric worse than the production baseline by more than 2 percentage points was a release blocker without explicit exception approval. Component 5 — production monitoring: I implemented monthly fairness audits in production — sampling 1,000 recent decisions per model and measuring protected group metrics against actual outcomes where ground truth was available (loan repayment outcomes, insurance claims, and hiring success).

**Result:** The programme identified that the insurance premium model had an indirect age discrimination issue — a proxy variable (years since first driving licence) was heavily correlated with age and was the fourth most important feature. Legal confirmed this required remediation. The recruitment model's monthly audit detected a fairness degradation after a model update — the false negative rate for women in technical roles had increased by 6 percentage points without the team noticing. Both were caught and corrected before the issues were visible to external parties or regulators.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Can define bias and run a basic demographic performance comparison |
| **Mid** | Implements fairness metrics across protected groups, identifies proxy variables, and presents findings with legal context |
| **Senior** | Designs and owns the full bias testing programme — metric selection framework, proxy audit methodology, release gates, and production monitoring — across multiple models and use cases |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Equalised odds** | A fairness criterion requiring equal true positive rates and false positive rates across demographic groups |
| **Demographic parity** | A fairness criterion requiring equal positive prediction rates across groups — a weaker standard than equalised odds |
| **Proxy variable** | A feature that correlates with a protected characteristic, enabling indirect discrimination even without explicit use of the protected attribute |
| **Fairness evaluation report** | A structured document summarising a model's performance metrics broken down by protected group — the gate artefact for a model release |
| **Production fairness audit** | A scheduled evaluation of deployed model decisions against actual outcomes, measuring protected group metrics on recent real-world data |
| **Per-group accuracy** | Model accuracy reported separately for each demographic group — revealing disparate performance hidden by aggregate accuracy metrics |
| **Exception approval** | A governance process allowing a release to proceed despite a failing gate condition, requiring explicit senior sign-off and a documented remediation plan |
