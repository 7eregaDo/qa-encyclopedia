## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can make and execute an architectural decision around observability tooling — balancing build versus buy, managing adoption across teams, and ensuring the platform produces actionable signals rather than becoming a dashboard that nobody trusts. At senior level, tooling decisions require both technical and organisational thinking.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My organisation's data platform had grown to 60 pipelines across 12 domain teams. Individual teams had built ad-hoc monitoring — some using Great Expectations, some using custom SQL jobs, some with nothing. There was no portfolio-level view of data health, alert routing was inconsistent, and the data team was spending significant time responding to complaints from downstream consumers rather than proactively detecting issues.

**Task:** I was asked to evaluate data observability platforms and make a recommendation, then lead implementation.

**Action:** I ran a structured evaluation. Requirements: I interviewed eight domain teams and three downstream consumer teams to identify the highest-value monitoring capabilities — freshness, volume anomaly detection, schema change alerting, and lineage-based impact assessment were consistently top-ranked. Build vs buy: I evaluated three commercial platforms (Monte Carlo, Acceldata, and Bigeye) against building on top of Great Expectations with custom anomaly detection. The commercial platforms offered ML-based anomaly detection (learning thresholds from historical data rather than requiring manual threshold setting) and native lineage integration — both of which would have taken significant engineering time to build. I recommended Monte Carlo based on native Snowflake and dbt integration, the quality of its lineage graph, and per-seat pricing that fit our budget. Implementation: I designed a phased rollout — starting with the five highest-incident pipelines to demonstrate value before asking all 12 teams to adopt. I defined a standard monitor taxonomy (freshness SLA, volume floor, schema change alert, and null rate threshold) that every pipeline was required to implement, with domain teams free to add additional monitors. I wrote a one-page alert response guide and held onboarding sessions for each team. I also set up a weekly data quality review in which open alerts were triaged with the owning teams — preventing alert fatigue by ensuring every alert was acted on.

**Result:** Within eight weeks, all 60 pipelines had the standard four monitors active. The ML-based volume anomaly detection caught a pipeline failure in week three that a fixed threshold would have missed — the pipeline's volume had dropped by 40% from a seasonally low baseline, which was within a fixed threshold but flagged correctly by the learned model. The weekly quality review reduced alert response time from 14 hours to 3 hours. After six months, downstream consumer complaints about undiscovered data issues dropped by 71%.

| Level | Expected Depth |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Data observability platform** | A commercial or open-source system providing automated monitoring of data freshness, volume, schema, and distribution — typically with ML-based anomaly detection and lineage integration |
| **ML-based anomaly detection** | Threshold-free monitoring that learns the expected range for a metric from historical data and alerts on statistically unusual deviations — more adaptive than fixed thresholds |
| **Monitor taxonomy** | A defined, consistent set of monitor types that every pipeline in the platform is required to implement — ensuring baseline coverage without mandating every possible check |
| **Alert fatigue** | The condition where too many alerts, or alerts without clear actions, cause teams to stop responding to monitoring signals — negating the value of the observability investment |
| **Monte Carlo** | A commercial data observability platform providing ML-based anomaly detection, data lineage, and automated incident routing — widely used in enterprise Snowflake environments |
| **Per-seat pricing** | A commercial pricing model charging per user rather than per pipeline or per data volume — relevant to total cost of ownership calculations in platform selection |
| **Phased rollout** | An adoption strategy that begins with a subset of high-value use cases to demonstrate value before requiring organisation-wide adoption |
