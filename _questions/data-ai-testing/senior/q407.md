## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can design monitoring at scale — across multiple models with different risk profiles, update frequencies, and data sources — rather than solving the problem for a single model in isolation. At senior level, the challenge is making the monitoring programme sustainable and actionable, not just technically correct.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My organisation had 11 ML models in production spanning fraud detection, demand forecasting, personalisation, and content moderation. Each team had built its own monitoring — or none at all. Two models had no monitoring, four had basic output distribution alerts with no defined response, and five had varying approaches with inconsistent thresholds. A portfolio-level view of model health did not exist.

**Task:** I was asked to design and implement a unified drift monitoring programme covering all 11 models, with consistent standards and actionable alert routing.

**Action:** I designed the programme in four stages. Stage 1 — model criticality tiering: I classified all 11 models into three tiers based on business impact of degradation. Tier 1 (four models): direct revenue or safety impact — fraud detection, pricing model, content moderation. Required real-time monitoring with a 1-hour alert SLA. Tier 2 (five models): significant but indirect business impact — personalisation, demand forecasting. Daily monitoring with a 24-hour alert SLA. Tier 3 (two models): low-impact internal tools. Weekly monitoring, email alert only. Stage 2 — monitoring method selection by model type: For supervised classification models (fraud, content moderation): input feature PSI weekly, output class distribution daily, and monthly accuracy audit against labelled ground truth. For regression models (demand forecasting, pricing): feature PSI weekly, prediction mean and variance daily, and monthly MAPE audit against actuals. For unsupervised and ranking models (personalisation): output distribution and diversity metrics weekly, business metric proxy (click-through rate, engagement) daily. Stage 3 — alert routing and response playbook: Every alert fired to the owning team's Slack channel and to a central model health dashboard. Each tier had a defined response playbook: Tier 1 alerts required acknowledgement within 1 hour and a root cause assessment within 4 hours; unacknowledged Tier 1 alerts escalated to engineering management. I wrote a one-page response guide for each model documenting the likely causes of each alert type and the standard response options (investigate, retrain, roll back). Stage 4 — centralised dashboard: I built a model health dashboard in Grafana showing the current drift status of all 11 models, their last monitoring run timestamp, and any open alerts — giving engineering leadership a single-page view of the portfolio's health.

**Result:** Within the first month of operation, the programme detected three previously unknown drift situations: one Tier 1 model (fraud) had significant input feature drift that had been developing for six weeks undetected, and two Tier 2 models had output distribution shifts. The fraud model retraining was expedited after the alert, recovering detection accuracy that had been quietly degrading. The centralised dashboard was reviewed in the monthly engineering leadership meeting, making model health a standing agenda item for the first time.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Model criticality tiering** | A classification of models by business impact of degradation — used to set monitoring frequency, alert SLAs, and response requirements |
| **PSI (Population Stability Index)** | A measure of distributional shift between two populations — values above 0.2 indicate significant drift requiring investigation |
| **MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)** | A regression model accuracy metric measuring the average percentage difference between predictions and actuals — used in periodic accuracy audits |
| **Response playbook** | A documented guide specifying the likely causes and standard responses for each type of drift alert for a specific model |
| **Alert SLA** | A defined maximum time for acknowledging and beginning investigation of a drift alert — based on the model's criticality tier |
| **Model health dashboard** | A centralised view of drift status, monitoring execution, and open alerts across all production models — enabling portfolio-level quality visibility |
| **Output distribution** | The statistical pattern of a model's predictions over time — shifts in this distribution are an early signal of drift even before ground truth labels are available |
