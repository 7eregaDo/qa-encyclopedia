## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you understand shadow mode as a distinct validation technique — different from canary deployment in that it carries zero user risk, and different from offline evaluation in that it uses real production inputs at full scale. At senior level, they want to see that you can design the comparison methodology, not just describe the concept.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My team was replacing a three-year-old gradient boosting fraud detection model with a new deep learning model. Offline evaluation showed the new model had a 4-point improvement in recall at equivalent precision. However, the fraud team was concerned that offline evaluation used a static historical test set — the new model had never been exposed to the full variety of live production traffic, including novel fraud patterns and edge cases not represented in training data.

**Task:** I was asked to design a shadow mode validation that would give the fraud team confidence in the new model's production behaviour before any traffic cutover.

**Action:** I designed the shadow mode test in three components. Component 1 — infrastructure: I added a shadow prediction service that received a copy of every incoming prediction request, ran it through the new model, and logged the result to a separate Kafka topic without returning it to the caller. The production model continued to serve all live traffic. The shadow predictions were ingested into a comparison table in Snowflake alongside the corresponding production model predictions. Component 2 — comparison methodology: I defined four comparison dimensions. Agreement rate: what percentage of requests did both models predict identically? A high disagreement rate warranted investigation even if neither model was objectively wrong. Prediction distribution comparison: I used PSI to compare the score distributions of both models across the full traffic volume — a significant distributional shift between the new and existing model warranted review. Disagreement case analysis: for every request where the models disagreed by more than 0.3 in their fraud probability score, I sampled 200 cases and routed them to the fraud operations team for manual review — assessing which model's judgement was more consistent with domain expertise. Ground truth lag evaluation: fraud labels arrive with a lag of 7–30 days (when disputes are resolved). I designed a delayed evaluation report that matched shadow predictions against ground truth labels as they arrived, computing precision and recall for the shadow model on real production outcomes. Component 3 — duration and decision criteria: I ran the shadow test for 28 days — long enough to accumulate sufficient ground truth labels for statistical significance. I defined four cutover criteria: ground truth recall for the shadow model must exceed the production model by at least 2 points; false positive rate must not exceed the production model by more than 0.5 points; disagreement case review must show shadow model judgement preferred by fraud analysts in at least 55% of reviewed cases; no class of transaction must show a significant quality regression.

**Result:** After 28 days, three of four criteria were met. The ground truth evaluation showed the new model's recall was 5.1 points higher. However, the disagreement case review found the shadow model was systematically less accurate on a specific transaction category — international wire transfers — where it had been underrepresented in training data. The cutover was proceeded with an exception: the new model was not used for international wire transfers until a targeted retraining on that segment was complete.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Shadow mode** | A deployment pattern where a new model receives a copy of production traffic and logs predictions without serving them to users — enabling zero-risk production-scale validation |
| **Agreement rate** | The proportion of requests where the shadow and production models produce equivalent predictions — a signal of how different the two models' behaviour is |
| **Ground truth lag** | The delay between a model prediction and the availability of the actual outcome label — relevant for fraud models where dispute resolution takes days to weeks |
| **Disagreement case analysis** | A review of requests where the shadow and production model predictions diverge significantly — used to assess which model's judgement is more consistent with domain expertise |
| **Cutover criterion** | A defined, pre-specified condition that must be met before traffic is switched from the existing to the new model — the decision gate for shadow mode validation |
| **Segment-level regression** | A quality degradation affecting a specific subset of inputs — not visible in aggregate metrics but discoverable through disaggregated analysis |
| **PSI (Population Stability Index)** | A metric measuring distributional shift between two populations — used here to compare prediction score distributions between shadow and production models |
