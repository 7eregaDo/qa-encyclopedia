## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can connect engineering testing practice to regulatory obligations — understanding not just what to test, but what evidence to produce, in what format, and retained for how long. At senior level, compliance testing is not a legal team hand-off — it is an engineering discipline with specific technical requirements.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My organisation was deploying an AI-powered credit decisioning tool in the EU market. Legal confirmed the tool was a high-risk AI system under the EU AI Act — triggering mandatory conformity assessment, technical documentation, and post-market surveillance obligations before it could be placed on the market. The engineering team had no experience with regulatory compliance testing and had assumed legal would handle it.

**Task:** I was asked to translate the EU AI Act's requirements for high-risk AI systems into a concrete testing programme with defined activities, responsible parties, and evidence artefacts.

**Action:** I mapped the Act's requirements to five testing domains, each with defined test activities and evidence outputs. Domain 1 — accuracy and robustness: The Act requires demonstration of appropriate accuracy and robustness against adversarial inputs. Test activities: evaluation on a representative test set with documented demographic breakdowns, adversarial robustness testing using input perturbation, and a comparison against the accuracy threshold stated in the technical documentation. Evidence: evaluation report with per-group metrics, robustness test results, and the signed-off accuracy threshold. Domain 2 — data governance: The Act requires that training, validation, and test data be subject to data governance practices ensuring relevance, representativeness, and freedom from errors. Test activities: training data quality report (schema validation, completeness check, bias audit), validation set representativeness assessment, and a data lineage record. Evidence: data quality report, data lineage documentation, DPO sign-off. Domain 3 — human oversight: The Act requires that high-risk AI systems can be effectively overseen and overridden by humans. Test activities: I explicitly tested the override mechanism — verified that a human operator could reject any model recommendation at the point of decision, that the system logged overrides with the operator's ID, and that overriding the model produced no system errors or unexpected state changes. Evidence: override test results, audit log samples. Domain 4 — transparency documentation: The Act requires technical documentation sufficient to demonstrate compliance, retained for 10 years. I designed a model card template capturing all required elements — intended purpose, training data description, performance metrics, known limitations, and instructions for use — and made its completion a mandatory release gate activity. Domain 5 — post-market surveillance: The Act requires ongoing monitoring of deployed AI performance. I implemented quarterly accuracy audits on a sample of real decisions with confirmed outcomes, and defined a serious incident reporting threshold — any instance of model malfunction causing demonstrable harm required notification to the relevant national authority within 15 days.

**Result:** The conformity assessment package — compiled from the evidence artefacts of all five testing domains — was submitted to the notified body and approved without revision. The audit log of testing activities was cited as unusually comprehensive by the reviewer. One enterprise customer's procurement team asked for access to the technical documentation as part of their AI governance due diligence — the model card was shared directly, reducing the procurement cycle by three weeks.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Conformity assessment** | A formal evaluation process demonstrating that a high-risk AI system meets the EU AI Act's requirements — required before the system can be placed on the EU market |
| **Notified body** | An independent organisation designated by an EU member state to conduct conformity assessments for high-risk AI systems |
| **Technical documentation** | A mandated record under the EU AI Act including the AI system's design, training data, performance, and risk management — retained for 10 years |
| **Post-market surveillance** | An ongoing obligation to monitor a deployed AI system's performance and report serious incidents to the relevant national authority |
| **Human oversight mechanism** | A designed and tested capability allowing a human operator to review, correct, or override the AI system's output at any decision point |
| **Serious incident** | A malfunction or defect in a high-risk AI system that causes or could cause death, injury, or significant harm — triggering a notification obligation to national authorities |
| **Model card** | A structured transparency document for a deployed model — serving as both a quality artefact and a component of the regulatory technical documentation |
