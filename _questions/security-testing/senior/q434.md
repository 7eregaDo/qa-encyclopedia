## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you understand PCI-DSS as a technical testing discipline, not just an administrative compliance exercise. The standard has specific, testable technical requirements — and a QA engineer who can design tests that produce PCI-DSS evidence, scope cardholder data environments correctly, and understand the difference between SAQ levels adds significant value to any organisation processing payment cards.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My organisation was processing card payments through a payment form embedded in our web application. We were approaching our annual PCI-DSS assessment. I was asked to lead the technical testing component — specifically to verify the controls required for our SAQ A-EP (self-assessment questionnaire for e-commerce merchants with partially outsourced card processing).

**Task:** Design and execute the technical security tests required for SAQ A-EP compliance, producing evidence suitable for the QSA review.

**Action:** I organised the testing around four SAQ A-EP control areas. Cardholder data environment (CDE) scoping: my first task was confirming our CDE scope was correctly minimised. I mapped all systems that touched cardholder data — the payment form page, the server that served it, the systems that received the authorisation response. I confirmed that primary account numbers (PANs) were never transmitted to our servers — the tokenisation was handled entirely by the payment provider's hosted fields. This confirmed SAQ A-EP rather than SAQ D scope — a significant reduction in compliance burden. Secure transmission: I verified that the payment form page was served exclusively over TLS 1.2 or above — I used `testssl.sh` to confirm no TLS 1.0 or 1.1 negotiation was possible. I also verified the payment provider's iframe was loaded from an HTTPS origin and that mixed content was blocked. Vulnerability management: SAQ A-EP requires quarterly external vulnerability scanning by an Approved Scanning Vendor (ASV). I reviewed the ASV scan reports for the previous four quarters to confirm they showed no unresolved high-severity findings. I also ran an internal authenticated scan against the payment form page's server to verify it had no critical vulnerabilities. Access control and logging: I verified that access to the systems serving the payment page was restricted to named individuals with business need, that privileged access was logged in CloudTrail, and that logs were retained for 12 months with 3 months immediately available. Web application firewall: SAQ A-EP requires a WAF protecting the payment page. I tested the WAF was active and blocking common attack payloads — a SQLi attempt, an XSS payload, and a path traversal — all of which returned 403 responses from the WAF before reaching the application.

**Result:** Two gaps found: TLS 1.1 was still negotiable on one server in the payment page's origin (required configuration fix) and WAF logging was not retained for the required 12 months (configuration fix). Both were remediated before the QSA review. The assessment passed with no exceptions on the technical controls. I produced a compliance evidence pack — one folder per requirement with timestamped test outputs — which the QSA reviewed without requesting additional evidence.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)** | A set of security requirements for organisations that process, store, or transmit payment card data — mandated by card networks and enforced through assessments |
| **SAQ (Self-Assessment Questionnaire)** | A PCI-DSS compliance form completed by merchants who do not require a full QSA audit — different SAQ types apply based on how card data is handled |
| **SAQ A-EP** | The PCI-DSS self-assessment for e-commerce merchants whose payment page is partially outsourced but served from their own servers |
| **CDE (Cardholder Data Environment)** | All systems that store, process, or transmit cardholder data or are connected to systems that do — defining the scope of PCI-DSS compliance requirements |
| **Tokenisation** | The replacement of a primary account number with a non-sensitive substitute token, allowing payment processing without the merchant handling the actual card number |
| **ASV (Approved Scanning Vendor)** | A company certified by the PCI Security Standards Council to perform quarterly external vulnerability scans required under PCI-DSS |
| **QSA (Qualified Security Assessor)** | A PCI-DSS certified assessor who conducts on-site compliance assessments for merchants and service providers who cannot self-assess |
| **Mixed content** | The loading of HTTP resources (scripts, iframes) within an HTTPS page — which downgrades the page's security and is prohibited for pages handling payment data |
