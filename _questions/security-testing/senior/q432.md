## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can act as the internal owner of an external pen test engagement — not just hand over a URL and wait for a report. At senior level, the value of an external test depends heavily on how well the internal QE scopes, prepares, communicates, and follows through. They want to see that you treat the pen tester as a partner, not a vendor.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** My company ran an annual external penetration test. The previous year's engagement had produced a 40-page report with 22 findings, but only 8 had been remediated by the next engagement — the report had been filed rather than acted on, and some findings were rejected by developers as false positives without proper investigation. I was asked to take ownership of the annual engagement and make it more effective.

**Task:** Redesign how the company ran its external pen test engagement — from scoping through findings remediation — to produce durable security improvement.

**Action:** I changed four aspects of how the engagement was run. Scoping: instead of giving the pen testers "the application," I worked with them in a scoping call to focus the engagement on three specific high-risk areas: the payment flow, the admin API, and the new SSO integration — areas our internal testing had not covered deeply and that presented the highest business risk. I provided the OpenAPI spec, the admin user credentials, and the SSO metadata endpoint. This concentrated effort where it had the most value. Pre-test preparation: I prepared a test environment with pre-seeded data representing all user roles and tenants, resolved the authentication setup questions in advance, and provided a context document: the system architecture, the trust model, and the three business-risk scenarios we most wanted tested. This eliminated the first-day setup friction that had consumed two days of the previous engagement. Collaborative triage: rather than receiving a final report, I arranged daily 30-minute check-ins during the engagement where the pen tester walked me through their findings in real time. I participated in distinguishing confirmed vulnerabilities from false positives, asked for clarification on reproduction steps, and provided context that sometimes changed the severity assessment. When one reported finding turned out to be a known accepted risk, I explained why — this saved the pen tester time pursuing it further. Findings to remediation programme: at the end of the engagement, I ran a joint review session with the pen tester and the engineering leads. Each finding was assigned an owner, a severity rating agreed by both parties, and a remediation deadline. I tracked remediation in the team's backlog alongside functional work and provided a written attestation to the pen tester when each finding was fixed — which they verified against their reproduction steps.

**Result:** 11 findings, all genuine (zero false positives in the final report — a significant improvement). 10 of 11 were remediated within the agreed deadlines. The one outstanding finding was a third-party dependency with no available fix — accepted as a tracked risk with a review date. The following year's engagement opened with the pen tester commenting that our preparation was the best they had seen from a client.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Provides access and credentials to the pen testing team when asked |
| **Mid** | Participates in scoping discussions; triages findings with developers; tracks remediation in the defect system |
| **Senior** | Owns the full engagement lifecycle — scoping to maximise value, preparing the test environment, running collaborative triage, managing remediation as a programme, and providing attestation of fixes to the pen testing firm |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Penetration test scope** | The defined set of systems, components, and attack scenarios included in a penetration testing engagement — scoping too broadly dilutes depth, too narrowly misses risk |
| **Collaborative triage** | The practice of reviewing penetration test findings with the tester in real time rather than receiving a static report — reducing false positives and improving finding quality |
| **Remediation attestation** | A written confirmation to the pen testing firm that a specific finding has been fixed, enabling them to verify and close the finding |
| **Pre-test context document** | A document provided to the pen testing team describing the system architecture, trust model, and priority attack scenarios — reducing setup time and directing effort |
| **False positive (pen test)** | A pen test finding that does not represent a genuine vulnerability in the production application — arising from test environment differences, misunderstood functionality, or incorrect tester assumptions |
| **Engagement lifecycle** | The full arc of a penetration testing engagement from initial scoping through findings remediation and re-verification |
| **Annual penetration test** | A periodic, comprehensive security assessment by an external specialist — required by many compliance frameworks as evidence of independent security assurance |
| **Accepted risk** | A documented decision to tolerate a known vulnerability because remediation is unavailable or disproportionately costly, with a defined review date and compensating controls |
