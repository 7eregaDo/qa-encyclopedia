## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can lead threat modelling for systems too complex for a single STRIDE session — where the attack surface spans multiple services, trust boundaries, and external integrations, and where business risk must inform the prioritisation of which threats to test first. At senior level, they want structured methodology and evidence that the output influenced engineering decisions.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** I was asked to lead threat modelling for a new open banking platform that would allow third-party developers to build applications using our customers' financial data, via OAuth 2.0 APIs. The system involved a developer portal, an authorisation server, a consent management service, three data APIs, and two legacy core banking system integrations. STRIDE on a whiteboard would not produce the depth of analysis needed for a system of this risk profile.

**Task:** Design and facilitate a threat modelling programme for the open banking platform that would produce a prioritised, risk-quantified threat register and drive both security test scope and architecture decisions.

**Action:** I applied PASTA across seven stages over three sessions spanning two weeks. Stage 1 — business objectives: I worked with the product and legal teams to define the three highest-consequence security outcomes to avoid — customer financial data exfiltration, unauthorised payment initiation, and regulatory breach of the FCA's Open Banking security requirements. This anchored every subsequent threat to business risk, not just technical possibility. Stage 2 — technical scope: I produced a component inventory with data classifications for each data flow. Stage 3 — application decomposition: I built a data flow diagram covering all twelve trust boundary crossings in the platform, using OWASP Threat Dragon for diagramming and version control. Stage 4 — threat analysis: for each trust boundary, I identified threat actors (malicious third-party developers, compromised customer devices, internal actors), their capabilities, and the threats they could execute. I used MITRE ATT&CK for reference rather than generating threats freehand. Stage 5 — vulnerability analysis: I mapped each threat to the specific technical conditions that would enable it — existing weaknesses, misconfiguration risks, or design gaps. Stage 6 — attack simulation: for the three highest-risk threats, I built attack trees showing the specific steps an attacker would need to execute. This revealed that the highest-risk attack chain required compromising a third-party developer's client credentials — a risk the team had not discussed. Stage 7 — risk and impact analysis: I scored each threat using CVSS environmental metrics calibrated to our business context and produced a prioritised register of 34 threats, of which 6 were classified high risk requiring architecture-level mitigations.

**Result:** Three architecture decisions changed as a direct result of the threat model: dynamic client registration was removed from the initial scope (attack surface reduction), the consent management service was isolated into its own network segment, and a third-party developer credential rotation requirement was added to the API terms of service. The threat register became the input to the security test plan — each high and medium threat had a corresponding test case. The FCA's technical assessment of the platform reviewed the threat model document and cited it as evidence of appropriate security design.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Can participate in a STRIDE session and contribute threats when prompted |
| **Mid** | Facilitates STRIDE threat modelling for a feature; translates threats into security requirements and test cases |
| **Senior** | Leads PASTA or risk-based threat modelling for complex multi-component systems; produces attack trees; aligns threat analysis to business objectives; drives architecture decisions from threat model output; presents findings to regulators |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **PASTA (Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis)** | A risk-centric threat modelling framework that aligns threat identification with business objectives, performing threat analysis through seven stages culminating in attack simulation |
| **Attack tree** | A hierarchical diagram representing how an attacker could achieve a goal, with the root node being the attack objective and branches representing alternative attack paths |
| **MITRE ATT&CK** | A publicly maintained knowledge base of adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures used as a reference for identifying realistic threats |
| **Trust boundary** | A point in a system where data passes between components with different trust levels — every boundary crossing is a potential security control requirement |
| **Risk quantification** | The assignment of a severity score to a threat based on its likelihood and potential business impact — enabling prioritised remediation and test scope decisions |
| **OWASP Threat Dragon** | An open-source threat modelling tool for creating and version-controlling data flow diagrams and threat registers |
| **Dynamic client registration** | An OAuth 2.0 mechanism allowing third-party applications to register with the authorisation server programmatically — an attack surface reduction candidate in high-risk environments |
| **Threat register** | A structured document cataloguing identified threats with their associated risk scores, affected components, and mitigations — the primary output of a formal threat modelling exercise |
