## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you treat defect data as a quality signal rather than just a count of bugs. At mid level, they want to see that you can extract meaningful insight from defect data — not just report how many bugs were found, but what the data says about where the product is fragile, whether the testing is catching defects early enough, and whether quality is improving over time.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** I had been on a product team for six months and the engineering manager asked me to produce a quality report for the quarterly engineering review. The team had been tracking defects in Jira but nobody had ever analysed the data systematically — defects were managed individually, never looked at as a population.

**Task:** I needed to produce a quality report that gave the engineering team and manager actionable insight, not just a count of closed tickets.

**Action:** I extracted six months of defect data and computed four metrics. Defect density: I calculated the number of defects per feature area normalised by the number of stories shipped from each area. Three areas stood out with consistently high density — payment processing, search, and the admin panel. This identified where engineering investment in testing and code quality was most needed. Defect escape rate: I calculated what percentage of defects were found in production versus in pre-production testing. The rate was 22% — meaning roughly one in five defects was reaching production. The industry benchmark for a comparable product type was under 10%. This told me the testing was not catching enough. Detection phase: I categorised each defect by where it was first found — in unit testing, integration testing, system testing, UAT, or production. 71% were found in system testing, 22% in production, and only 7% in earlier phases. This confirmed the shift-left opportunity: the team was finding most defects late in the cycle. Defect age: I measured the average time between a defect being opened and closed, by severity. Critical defects were averaging 4.2 days to close — acceptable. Major defects were averaging 18 days — much longer than expected and indicating a backlog management problem. I presented the four metrics as a one-page dashboard with trend lines for each, and three specific recommendations: targeted exploratory testing investment in the three high-density areas, a shift-left initiative to move detection earlier, and a defect triage process to prevent major defects from ageing in the backlog.

**Result:** The engineering manager used the report in the quarterly review and approved a two-sprint shift-left initiative. Over the following quarter, the escape rate dropped from 22% to 14% and the detection phase distribution shifted — system testing proportion dropped to 58% as earlier phases caught more defects. The defect age report triggered a weekly defect triage meeting that cleared the major defect backlog within six weeks.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Can report how many defects were found and their severity; knows what escape rate means |
| **Mid** | Computes defect density, escape rate, detection phase distribution, and defect age; identifies trends and presents actionable insights to the engineering team |
| **Senior** | Designs the quality metrics framework for the team — defines what to measure and why, builds dashboards, connects metrics to business outcomes, and uses trend data to drive strategic quality investment decisions |

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Defect density** | The number of defects per unit of product (feature area, lines of code, or story points) — identifies where the product is most fragile |
| **Defect escape rate** | The proportion of defects that reach production rather than being caught in pre-production testing — the primary measure of testing effectiveness |
| **Detection phase** | The stage in the SDLC where a defect was first identified — used to assess whether testing is finding defects early enough |
| **Defect age** | The elapsed time between a defect being opened and closed — long age indicates backlog management or prioritisation problems |
| **Quality trend** | The direction of a metric over time — a declining escape rate indicates improving quality; a rising defect density in a stable area signals a new risk |
| **Defect triage** | A regular team process for reviewing open defects, assigning priorities, and preventing age accumulation |
| **Normalised density** | Defect count adjusted for the volume of work delivered — prevents high-output teams from appearing lower quality than lower-output teams |
