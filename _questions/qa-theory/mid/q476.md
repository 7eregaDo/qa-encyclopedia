## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can give credible estimates under uncertainty — not optimistic single numbers that stakeholders treat as commitments. Mid-level QEs are expected to own estimation for their testing scope, justify the basis of their estimates, and communicate the conditions under which they might be wrong. An engineer who says "I can't estimate without complete requirements" is less useful than one who can estimate with documented assumptions.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** I was asked to estimate the testing effort for a new release that included four user stories in the defined state, two stories still in design, and a dependency on a third-party integration whose API documentation was incomplete. The project manager wanted the estimate by end of day for a capacity planning meeting.

**Task:** I needed to produce a credible effort estimate within a few hours, covering known and uncertain scope, in a form that made my assumptions visible so stakeholders could make informed decisions.

**Action:** I used three estimation techniques in combination. Technique 1 — decomposition for known scope: for the four defined stories, I broke testing down into its component activities — test design, test execution, defect re-testing, and documentation. I estimated each activity based on story complexity (simple, medium, complex) using reference data from the past six sprints. The four stories produced a range of 11–15 hours, with the range reflecting variation in defect count from comparable stories historically. Technique 2 — analogy for the two design-phase stories: I identified the most similar stories from the past three months and used their actual effort as a basis, adding a 30% buffer for requirements uncertainty. This produced 6–10 hours for the two stories. Technique 3 — risk-adjusted estimate for the third-party integration: I explicitly called the integration out as a high-uncertainty item. I estimated the optimistic case (API works as expected, no surprises: 4 hours), the most likely case (minor integration issues, one round of debugging: 8 hours), and the pessimistic case (significant API behaviour divergence requiring test redesign: 16 hours). I presented the full range rather than the most likely number only. I compiled the estimate as a structured document: total range 21–41 hours, most likely 28–33 hours, with three named assumptions (complete API documentation received by date X, stories Y and Z entered sprint with defined acceptance criteria, no environment outages) and three named risks (each with an hour impact if they materialised).

**Result:** The project manager used the range to flag the integration as a capacity risk in the planning meeting. The team decided to de-scope the integration to a follow-up release, which reduced the estimate to 17–25 hours — manageable within the sprint capacity. Two of my three risks materialised: one story entered the sprint without final acceptance criteria, adding 2 hours. The actual effort was 24 hours, within the adjusted range.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Gives a single-point estimate based on gut feel; may struggle to justify the number when challenged |
| **Mid** | Uses decomposition and historical analogy to produce a range estimate with documented assumptions and named risks; communicates uncertainty explicitly to stakeholders |
| **Senior** | Designs the estimation framework for the team — calibration data collection, reference story library, uncertainty quantification model, and a retrospective process that improves estimate accuracy over time |

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Decomposition estimation** | Breaking a testing scope into component activities and estimating each separately — producing a more accurate total than estimating the whole |
| **Analogical estimation** | Using the actual effort of comparable past work as the basis for estimating similar future work |
| **Three-point estimation** | An estimation technique using optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic cases to produce a range — makes uncertainty explicit |
| **Estimation range** | A minimum-to-maximum effort span rather than a single point — more honest and more useful for capacity planning |
| **Estimation assumption** | A documented condition on which the estimate depends — if the assumption is wrong, the estimate changes |
| **Buffer** | Additional effort added to an estimate to account for uncertainty, risk, or historical over-optimism |
| **Calibration data** | Historical actual effort from completed work, used to improve the accuracy of future estimates by comparison |
