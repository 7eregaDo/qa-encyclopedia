## The Intent
The interviewer is checking whether you can treat the pipeline as an engineering system with its own economics. As organisations scale to hundreds of services and thousands of daily pipeline runs, CI costs become a significant budget line — and senior QEs who can optimise costs while maintaining quality provide dual value. At senior level, they want to see measurement, trade-off reasoning, and governance — not just anecdotal optimisations.

## The STAR Script
**Situation:** As senior QE for a 25-service platform running on GitHub Actions with self-hosted runners on AWS, I was asked to review the CI/CD infrastructure cost after the CTO noticed it had grown from £8,000 to £31,000 per month over 18 months — a 290% increase against a 150% growth in engineering headcount. The growth was not proportionate, indicating inefficiency rather than just scale.

**Task:** Identify the sources of disproportionate cost growth, implement targeted optimisations, and establish governance to prevent uncontrolled future growth.

**Action:** I approached the optimisation in three phases. Phase 1 — cost attribution and measurement: I instrumented the runner infrastructure with CloudWatch metrics to break down cost by service, pipeline stage, and job type. Findings: three services accounted for 47% of total compute time despite representing only 18% of engineering headcount. Two of these were running full E2E suites on every push — including pushes to inactive draft branches. One service had a parallelisation configuration that was spinning up 16 runners for a 3-minute test suite that ran faster than the runner provisioning overhead. Phase 2 — targeted optimisations: I implemented six specific changes. Branch-based execution filtering: E2E tests were restricted to run only on PRs targeting main and named release branches — removing draft branch and WIP branch execution saved 22% of compute. Right-sizing runners: I matched runner instance types to job requirements. Lint and unit test jobs ran on `t3.medium` instances; only E2E tests ran on `c5.xlarge`. This reduced the average per-runner-minute cost by 34% for the majority of jobs. Parallelisation efficiency: the over-parallelised service was reduced from 16 to 4 runners — the overhead of 16 runners provisioning sequentially was greater than the parallelisation benefit. Cache effectiveness: I audited dependency caching across all 25 services and found 8 services had cache configurations that were regenerating on every run due to incorrect cache key patterns. Fixing the cache keys eliminated redundant dependency installation across 40% of runs. Nightly-only scheduling for expensive suites: three services moved their performance test suites from post-merge to nightly, eliminating their most expensive stage from the PR pipeline entirely. Phase 3 — cost governance: I introduced monthly cost attribution reports by team — each squad received their pipeline cost for the month alongside a benchmark of cost-per-deployment compared to the platform average. Squads spending more than 150% of the average per deployment received a recommendation for review. I also introduced a pipeline cost impact requirement for any new pipeline stage addition — a one-line estimate of the added monthly cost, reviewed by the engineering lead.

**Result:** Monthly pipeline cost dropped from £31,000 to £17,400 within three months — a 44% reduction — with zero reduction in quality gate coverage or defect detection rate. The cost attribution reports prompted two squads to voluntarily optimise their pipelines without prompting. The governance process caught one proposal for a new pipeline stage that would have added £2,800/month — the team found a more targeted approach after seeing the cost estimate.

## Seniority Shift

| Level | Expected Depth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Entry** | Aware that CI/CD pipelines have infrastructure costs; follows existing pipeline configurations |
| **Mid** | Identifies obviously inefficient pipeline patterns; implements caching; adjusts parallelisation for a specific pipeline |
| **Senior** | Measures cost at service and stage granularity; implements systematic optimisations across the platform; introduces cost attribution governance; balances cost reduction against quality signal integrity |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cost attribution** | The breakdown of CI/CD infrastructure spend by team, service, or pipeline stage — the foundation for identifying disproportionate cost and driving ownership |
| **Branch-based execution filtering** | Configuring pipeline stages to run only on specific branch types (PR targeting main, named release branches) — preventing expensive stages from running on inactive or draft branches |
| **Right-sizing** | Matching compute instance type to the actual resource requirements of a job — preventing over-provisioning of expensive instances for lightweight tasks |
| **Cache effectiveness** | The proportion of CI runs that successfully retrieve dependencies from cache rather than downloading them — improved by correct cache key configuration |
| **Parallelisation overhead** | The fixed cost of provisioning, starting, and tearing down parallel runners — which can exceed the parallelisation benefit for short-duration test suites |
| **Cost-per-deployment** | A normalised pipeline cost metric expressing total pipeline spend divided by number of deployments — enabling comparison between teams with different deployment frequencies |
| **Pipeline cost governance** | An organisational process requiring cost impact estimates for new pipeline stages and regular attribution reporting by team — preventing uncontrolled pipeline cost growth |
| **Quality signal integrity** | The property that cost optimisations do not reduce the pipeline's ability to detect genuine defects — the constraint that bounds all cost reduction activity |
